Expressing unexpected closeness

Hace pocos días que te conozco y parece como si hiciera muchos años.

Native neural Spanish audio
Default: 0.85× One recording · one player
Natural English
I’ve only known you for a few days, but it feels as though it had been many years.
◆ Power chunk
parece como si + imperfect subjunctive
◇ Grammar insight
«Como si» introduces an unreal comparison. Spanish steps away from reality with the imperfect subjunctive: «hiciera».
↗ Native upgrade
Parece que nos conociéramos de toda la vida.
It feels as if we had known each other forever.
× Common mistake
como si hace muchos años
como si hiciera muchos años
◌ Pronunciation
Link «días_que» smoothly. Avoid inserting a full pause between the two words.
◎ Cultural feel
This sounds warm and emotionally direct. It fits a sincere, intimate conversation better than casual small talk.
✦ Memory hook
«Como si» opens an imaginary world. Once the comparison leaves reality, the verb enters the subjunctive.

Spanish micro-learning · 03
High intermediate
Changing your mind

Cuanto más lo pienso, más claro tengo que deberíamos esperar.

Native neural Spanish audio
Default: 0.85× One recording · one player
Natural English
The more I think about it, the clearer it becomes to me that we should wait.
◆ Power chunk
Cuanto más..., más...
◇ Grammar insight
This paired structure shows two things increasing together. The first clause creates the condition; the second gives the result.
↗ Native upgrade
Después de pensarlo bien, creo que lo más sensato sería esperar.
After thinking it through, I think the most sensible thing would be to wait.
× Common mistake
Mientras más lo pienso...
Cuanto más lo pienso...
◌ Pronunciation
Keep «más_lo» connected. Avoid a heavy pause after «más» in the first half.
◎ Cultural feel
In Spain, «cuanto más» is the safest polished form. «Mientras más» is common in many parts of Latin America.
✦ Memory hook
Picture two rising lines: more thought leads to more certainty.

Interrupting politely

◆ Power chunk
Perdona que + subjunctive
◇ Grammar insight
After «perdona que», Spanish normally uses the subjunctive because the speaker is reacting to an action they are performing.

The stress falls on «rrump» in «interrumpa». Keep the double r strong but brief.

This is direct enough to enter the conversation but respectful enough for meetings, family discussions, and formal settings.
✦ Memory hook
The apology opens a small door; the subjunctive lets the interruption pass through politely.

◆ Power chunk
Si estuviera..., empezaría...
◇ Grammar insight
Use imperfect subjunctive in the hypothetical «si» clause and conditional in the result.
↗ Native upgrade
Yo, en tu situación, iría paso a paso y evitaría complicarlo desde el principio.
In your situation, I would go step by step and avoid making it complicated from the start.

Framing advice as a personal hypothetical makes it sound supportive rather than commanding.
✦ Memory hook
The imaginary situation lives in the subjunctive; the imagined action lives in the conditional.

Expressing relief

Native neural Spanish audio

Natural English
Good thing you told me; otherwise, I would have arrived at the wrong place.
◆ Power chunk
Menos mal que...
◇ Grammar insight
«Habría llegado» is conditional perfect. It describes an unreal past result that did not happen.
↗ Native upgrade
Por suerte me lo dijiste a tiempo; de lo contrario, habría sido un desastre.
Luckily, you told me in time; otherwise, it would have been a disaster.
× Common mistake
Menos malo que...
Menos mal que...
◌ Pronunciation
Say «menos_mal» as one rhythmic unit. The expression is usually delivered quickly and with feeling.
◎ Cultural feel
«Menos mal» is extremely common in everyday Spanish and often carries more feeling than a literal «fortunately».
✦ Memory hook
Something bad almost happened, but there was ‘less bad’: «menos mal».
